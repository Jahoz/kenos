03 / The universe

Nine territories
in the void

The founding gesture has grown into an inhabited world. Everything below is real, live, and honors the same law: one reading only, never a return.

TERRITORY 01

The journey

The eye glides through the void at a stroke of a finger. The sky gives itself only to the traveler: each sector reveals itself on approach — the whole map exists nowhere.

TERRITORY 02

The reception field

Near your eye, a star is vivid and holdable. Beyond, it is a mute glimmer. The bottle at sea is earned by distance — to zoom is to approach.

TERRITORY 03

The comets

A read thought can be reborn: relaunched, it departs re-sealed for one new reader only — its phoenix trail crossing every orbit. The number of carriers is public, never the words.

TERRITORY 04

The constellations

Strangers write a blind poem (an exquisite corpse, the surrealists would say), one line each, continuing the line before theirs. Closed, the poem becomes an artifact: readable by all, forever sealed. Some constellations are born as songs — phrases of notes continued by ear, sung through space by the completed figure.

TERRITORY 05

The named system

Seven bodies carry names. The Moon soothes, Venus gathers whispered confessions, Polaris lights — fixed at the heart of the turning sky. Four wanderers drift farther out, silent: nothing orbits here. Not yet.

TERRITORY 06

The reliquary

A read artifact can be kept in your sky: seven objects at most, marked with an ember, beyond the moon. The eighth returns the oldest to the void. What you have read leaves a seven-day scar — never a byte leaves your device.

TERRITORY 07

The waves

The spatialized symphony: each wave born near you is heard at its place in the stereo field, then fades after sixty seconds. No words, only sound.

TERRITORY 08

The doors

A confidence can carry a borrowed voice — a track, a clip — sealed with it. At the revelation, thirty seconds play in the window; the door closes when the echo dies.

TERRITORY 09

The vestiges

Culture drifts through the void: shards of astronomy, etymologies, haikus — and real public-domain poems, signed. Rimbaud, Verlaine, Baudelaire. The curator sows them; a vestige is always re-readable.
